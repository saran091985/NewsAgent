# Style guide — "The Learning Kids" news show

This file is read by the AI every time it writes the scripts. Edit it freely:
change the rules, or swap the example stories for ones you like better.
Your full past scripts are in style/examples/.

## Voice
- The host is a 10-year-old: excited, warm, curious, talking to friends aged 8-14.
- Real news, told simply. Serious stories (wars, attacks, disasters) are covered honestly
  but calmly: say what happened, who is helping, and what people hope for. No gory details.
- Short sentences. Every hard word gets explained with an everyday comparison
  (a ceasefire is a giant "time-out"; the Strait of Hormuz is a narrow "water hallway";
  OPEC is a "global oil club"; 1,176 hectares is "like 1,600 soccer fields").
- Keep the real names, places and numbers from the article, then make big numbers easy to picture.
- 1-2 emojis per story, placed at the end of a sentence. Never more.
- Never start stories the same way. Do NOT open stories with "Hey kids!", "Did you know",
  or "Big news!". Do NOT end every story with a question to the viewers.

## Shape of one story
- A playful, specific title (not the newspaper headline), e.g.
  "Japan's Brave Firefighters vs. The Mega-Fire", "The Big Squeeze", "SpaceX's Giant Satellite Carpool".
- 2-4 bullet points, each starting with a short bold label that fits the story, e.g.
  "The Battle:", "The Ceasefire:", "The News:", "The Reason:", "The Score:", "Why it matters:",
  "Good News:", "Don't Worry:", "Fun Fact:".
- Optional explainer box for the one hardest word: "Wait, what's a Tariff?" followed by a
  1-2 sentence answer with an everyday comparison.

## Example stories (the style to copy)

**4. The Great Car Fee (Tariffs)**
- **The Rule:** Donald Trump announced a 25% "tariff" on cars coming from Europe.
- **Wait, what's a Tariff?** It's an extra tax or fee. If a car costs $100, you have to pay an extra $25 just to bring it into the country. It's meant to encourage people to buy cars made in the USA!

**2. Japan's Brave Firefighters vs. The Mega-Fire**
- **The Battle:** In northern Japan (a place called Iwate), one of the biggest wildfires in decades broke out! It's been burning for 11 whole days.
- **Superheroes in Action:** Over 1,000 firefighters are working like real-life superheroes to stop the flames.
- **The Size:** The fire burned over 1,176 hectares—that is like 1,600 soccer fields! Let's give a big cheer for the brave teams keeping the towns safe. 🚒

**1. The Iran-US Conflict: A Big Sigh of Relief?**
- **The Ceasefire:** The conflict with Iran is being "terminated" early! A ceasefire is basically a giant "Time-Out" where countries agree to stop fighting so they can talk instead of argue.
- **The Big Squeeze:** There was a standoff at the Strait of Hormuz. This is a very narrow "water hallway" in the ocean where big ships carry 20% of the whole world's oil! Because it was blocked, oil prices zoomed up like a rocket. 🚀
- **Good News:** Now that things are cooling down, there's hope that "water hallway" will open back up and gas prices will stop being so grumpy!

**7. Meet RAVEN: The AI Planet Hunter**
- **The Tool:** Scientists at the University of Warwick made a super-smart AI (Artificial Intelligence) named RAVEN.
- **The Mission:** RAVEN acts like a robot detective, looking through data from the TESS space telescope to find hidden planets.
- **The Score:** It just confirmed over 100 exoplanets! 31 of them are brand new discoveries.
- **Wait, what's an Exoplanet?** 🪐 An exoplanet is any planet outside our solar system. It doesn't circle our Sun; it circles a different star far, far away!

## Section openers (one line in quotes before each topic)
- "First up, let's look at what's happening across the big blue marble we call home!"
- "Now, let's zoom over to India, where the news is buzzing like a beehive!"
- "Space: the place where you can float like a marshmallow! Here's what's happening way up there."

## Intro and outro
- Intro: a fun greeting ("Testing, 1-2-3! Is this thing on? High-five, world! ✋"), tease 2-3 of
  today's biggest stories in a few words each, then "Buckle up, because the news starts... NOW!"
- Outro: "That's the news for today! Stay curious, keep exploring, and I'll see you next time!" 👋
